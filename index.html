What does it mean?

A combination counts groups, not arrangements.

👥

If you choose Alice and Ben for a team, that is the same team as Ben and Alice. So the order is not counted twice.

Remember: C(n, 0) = 1 and C(n, n) = 1. There is one way to choose nothing, and one way to choose everything.

Why these ideas matter

Repetition: distribute identical items

Suppose 5 identical tokens are assigned across 3 categories. The number of non-negative distributions is

C(5 + 3 − 1, 5) = C(7, 5) = 21

Multinomial coefficients: count category orders

Among 4 positions, suppose 2 are category A, 1 is B, and 1 is C. The distinct orders are

4! / (2! × 1! × 1!) = 12

Formula reminder

C(n, r) = n! / [r! (n − r)!]

Use combinations when:

  • You are selecting a group.
  • The order of selection does not matter.
  • Objects are distinct unless repetition is explicitly allowed.

1. Combination

A combination is a selection of r objects from n distinct objects in which the order of selection does not matter.

C(n, r) = n! / [r!(n − r)!], where 0 ≤ r ≤ n

Solved example: Select 3 students from 7 students.

C(7, 3) = 7! / (3! × 4!) = 35

So, 35 different groups can be selected.

2. Combination with repetition

A combination with repetition selects r items from n available types when a type may be selected more than once and the order does not matter.

C(n + r − 1, r) = (n + r − 1)! / [r!(n − 1)!]

Solved example: Choose 3 scoops from 4 ice-cream flavours, allowing flavours to repeat.

C(4 + 3 − 1, 3) = C(6, 3) = 20

So, 20 flavour selections are possible.

3. Multinomial theorem

The multinomial theorem expands a power of a sum of two or more terms. The coefficient counts the distinct arrangements of terms with specified powers.

(x₁ + ··· + xₖ)ⁿ = Σ [n! / (n₁!···nₖ!)] x₁ⁿ¹···xₖⁿᵏ, with n₁ + ··· + nₖ = n

Solved example: Find the coefficient of x²yz in (x + y + z)⁴.

4! / (2! × 1! × 1!) = 24 / 2 = 12

The required coefficient is 12.

Definitions of the other topics

4. Combination with restrictions

A restricted combination is a selection that must satisfy a condition, such as including a required person or choosing a fixed number from each group.

Example: A 3-person team with exactly 2 girls from 4 girls and 5 boys can be chosen in C(4,2) × C(5,1) = 30 ways.

5. Binomial coefficient and theorem

A binomial coefficient C(n,r) is the coefficient that counts ways to choose r objects from n. The binomial theorem expands a power of a two-term sum.

(x + y)ⁿ = Σ C(n,r)xⁿ⁻ʳyʳ

6. Graph theory and networks

Graph theory studies vertices and edges. In a complete simple undirected graph, each pair of distinct vertices has one edge.

Edges = C(n,2) = n(n−1)/2

7. Combinations with repetition and distributions

The stars-and-bars method counts ways to distribute identical items among distinct categories when categories may receive zero or more items.

Distribute m items to k categories: C(m+k−1,m)

8. Multinomial coefficient

A multinomial coefficient counts distinct arrangements of n positions containing repeated categories of sizes n₁, n₂, …, nₖ.

n! / (n₁! n₂! ··· nₖ!)
